The screening lab: recall you can defend

Your literature review has two numbers nobody writes down: how much of the relevant evidence you actually found, and how much of what you included actually belongs. This session makes both visible. You will run a 40-record screen with your own hands, watch recall climb from 4 of 12 to 11 of 12 as five practices come on - and then press the tempting button, the one that lets AI screen everything unverified, and watch two relevant studies vanish with perfectly reasonable-sounding excuses.

Part 1 · the standard

The screening pipeline, and its two honest numbers 7 min live

Every serious evidence synthesis - and the PRISMA 2020 statement makes this explicit for systematic reviews - reports a flow: how many records were identified, how many survived deduplication, how many were screened, how many were included. That flow is not bureaucracy. It is the audit trail that lets someone else judge whether your review can be trusted. Two quantities hide inside it.

LiveWhy "I searched thoroughly" is not a number4 min▶

In the lab you know the ground truth: exactly 12 of the 40 records genuinely answer the question. Real life never hands you that answer key - which is exactly why the practices matter. You cannot measure your real recall directly, so the discipline is to stack the moves that are known to raise it:

  • A structured search string with the field's synonyms. A naive keyword search finds only papers that share your vocabulary. In the lab, this single lever takes recall from 4/12 to 7/12.
  • Citation snowballing - backward through reference lists, forward through citing papers. It is how you find the paper that says "successive relearning" when you searched "retrieval practice". Lab effect: 7/12 to 9/12.
  • A known-set spot check. Before screening, write down 5 papers you already know must be in scope. If your search did not return all 5, your string is broken and you have learned it for free.
Real world

The vocabulary problem is not hypothetical. The same intervention appears in the literature as retrieval practice, the testing effect, practice testing, test-enhanced learning, low-stakes quizzing and successive relearning - six names, decades apart, different subfields. A reviewer who searched one term found one slice and honestly believed it was the field.

LiveWritten criteria: the unglamorous lever that beats the AI4 min▶

Inclusion criteria written down before screening - population, intervention, comparison, outcome - are what stop a Tuesday-you and a Friday-you disagreeing about the same abstract. In the lab they are worth two recovered studies AND all three ejected near-misses, the biggest combined jump on the board.

Criteria in your head

"Studies about retrieval practice in university courses" - so is a quasi-experiment in? A lab study with undergraduates but word lists instead of course content? Retention at one week? You will answer differently on different days, and never notice.

Criteria on paper (and in every AI prompt)

Include if ALL hold: (1) undergraduate students in a STEM course context; (2) retrieval practice vs a re-study or business-as-usual comparison; (3) an objective retention outcome measured at a delay; (4) experimental or quasi-experimental design with a comparison group. Exclude: K-12, lab-only word-list tasks, self-report outcomes, no comparison group, opinion or review without new data.

Notice the last line of the good version quietly handles four of the lab's traps. That is not luck - the traps are the standard failure modes, and written criteria are the standard defence.

Self-studyWhat PRISMA 2020 actually asks of you3 min read▶

PRISMA 2020 is a 27-item reporting checklist plus the flow diagram, for systematic reviews. You are not required to run a full systematic review to borrow its spine - even a thesis literature review gets more defensible when you can state your sources, your search string, your criteria, and your counts at each stage. What PRISMA cannot do is make the judgement calls for you: it standardises the reporting of your screen, not the quality of it.

If you are doing the real thing: the statement and its explanation paper are open access at prisma-statement.org, and your discipline may have its own variant (PRISMA-ScR for scoping reviews). Register the protocol (PROSPERO for health, OSF elsewhere) before you screen, not after - b5 returns to why the "before" matters.

Part 2 · the AI question

What AI screening buys, exactly 7 min live

Screening is the most automatable-looking task in research: thousands of abstracts, one yes/no each. Tools have earned real results here - ASReview's active-learning screener is open source with published validation studies, Elicit reports high agreement on structured extraction, and Covidence and Rayyan ship AI-assist features. The published evaluations share one condition: the workload savings hold when a human stays in the loop. The lab's trap button shows you why that condition is not negotiable.

LiveThe failure mode: plausibly wrong, invisibly wrong4 min▶

When the trap button is on, the lab excludes two genuinely relevant studies with these reasons:

  • "Excluded - retention interval unclear from abstract."
  • "Excluded - appears to concern assessment policy rather than a learning intervention."

Read them again. Neither is stupid. Each is the kind of call a tired human screener might also make - which is exactly the problem. A wrong AI exclusion does not look wrong; it looks like a decision. You only catch it by checking, and if you never check, your review is missing evidence and reporting confidence.

The symmetric failure is quieter still: three records that are not evidence - an opinion piece, a narrative overview with no data, an uncontrolled vendor case study - come back marked include with confident reasons. Nothing crashes. The meters just get worse.

Self-studyThe verification protocol that keeps the savings4 min read▶

The workable middle - and the shape behind tools like ASReview's active learning - is triage, not delegation:

  • Give the AI your written criteria verbatim. Its screen is only as good as the rules you hand it. No criteria, same wobble as you on a bad day - the lab demonstrates this if you turn AI on without the criteria lever.
  • Ask for a confidence-ranked sort, not a verdict. Screen the "likely include" and "uncertain" bands yourself; spot-check a sample of the "likely exclude" band. Most of the reading you save was always in the confident-exclude tail.
  • Log every AI exclusion with its reason. An auditable screen means someone can disagree with a specific decision, not with a black box.
  • Dual-screen a calibration sample first. Run 50 records yourself and compare. If you and the tool disagree on more than a few, your criteria are ambiguous - fix the criteria, not the tool.

If you are running a formal systematic review, check the journal's and Cochrane's current stance on automation in screening before you rely on it, and report the tool, version and protocol in your methods. Norms here are moving; your methods section is where honesty about that lives.

Demo 1 of 2

Climb the ladder ★ 12 min · everyone runs it

Six steps, one practice at a time. At every step, say out loud what the recall meter will do before you click - being wrong here is the useful part.

Step 0: naive keyword search, no written criteria. 27 records, recall 4/12, precision 44%. Look inside the corpus table: a borderline quasi-experiment wobbled out, three near-misses wobbled in, and a preprint twin is being counted twice.

Step 1: add the boolean string. Recall 7/12 - three studies you were simply never seeing. Nothing about your screening got smarter; your net got wider.

Step 2: add snowballing. 9/12. The two new finds use vocabulary no search string guessed.

Step 3: dedup. Recall holds, precision jumps to 75% - the twins are gone. Cheap, mechanical, skipped surprisingly often.

Step 4: written criteria. 11/12 and precision 100%. The borderline studies come back, the near-misses leave. Paperwork beat the algorithm - remember this rung.

Step 5: AI screening with human verification. Same 11/12, same 100% - and hand-screened abstracts drop from 35 to 11. That is the honest offer: time, not truth.

Demo 2 of 2

Spring the trap ★ 10 min · explore

Free play. Build the full stack, then press the red button and read the damage report. Then try the subtler experiments: AI verification WITHOUT written criteria (the wobble comes back - the AI is screening against rules that do not exist), or the trap with a weak search (the failures compound).

What is constructed and what is computed The 40 records are constructed for this exercise - realistic-shaped titles around a real research question, not real citations, never to be cited. The meters are not scripted: recall, precision, the PRISMA counts and the workload figure are computed live from the record set and your lever state, every click. The real literature on retrieval practice is real and rich - start with Roediger & Karpicke (2006), Dunlosky et al. (2013), and the Adesope et al. (2017) meta-analysis.
Homework

Try it on your own review - this week ◐ 40-50 min total

  • Write inclusion criteria for your current project in the four-part form (population, intervention/exposure, comparison, outcome) plus explicit exclusions. If you cannot, that is a finding about your research question, not about your writing.
  • Run the known-set spot check: list 5 papers your review must include, run your actual search string, and count how many it returns. Fix the string until it finds all 5.
  • Take 20 abstracts you have already screened and re-screen them against your now-written criteria. Count the decisions that changed. That number is your personal wobble rate.
  • If you use an AI screening tool, run the calibration protocol from Part 2 on 50 records and write down the disagreement rate before trusting it with the rest.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · In the lab, which lever produces the biggest combined gain in recall AND precision?

The boolean string is the biggest pure-recall jump (4 to 7), but written criteria move both meters at once: recall 9 to 11 and precision to 100%. The AI rung changes neither number - it cuts the reading. The unglamorous paperwork lever is the strongest one on the board, which is the session's quiet thesis.

2 · Why is the unverified-AI failure mode specifically dangerous, compared with a crash or an error message?

"Excluded - retention interval unclear from abstract" reads like a decision, not a malfunction. Wrong exclusions look identical to right ones unless someone checks, and the studies they remove are precisely the evidence you never knew existed. That is why every published evaluation showing real savings keeps a human verifying the boundary.

3 · The best stack in the lab tops out at 11 of 12. What does the missing study teach?

The twelfth study lives in an unindexed conference proceeding. No search lever reaches it, because searching only covers what is indexed. The real-world equivalents are trial registries, theses, preprints outside your databases, and asking people in the field. The defensible move is not pretending to completeness - it is stating the coverage limit in your methods.
