Part 1 · the contract

One question, the whole cycle, receipts attached 8 min live

The capstone contract has three clauses. First: the question is real - your thesis chapter, your next paper, your grant aim - never a toy. Second: it runs the whole cycle, even the stages you would normally rush. Third: every stage leaves an artifact, so the finished trail can be audited by someone who was not there. Self-studyChoosing the question - the three-part fitness test3 min read▶

The right capstone question passes three tests. It is yours - a live thesis chapter, paper or aim, so the artifacts you build this week are work you needed to do anyway. It is answerable at slice scale - a question whose literature you can search and screen in a day, not a field survey; narrow beats grand here. And it touches data or code somewhere - if your project is purely theoretical, pick the empirical corner of it, because days 2 and 3 need something to extract and something to run. If no current project fits, use your last completed analysis and rebuild its trail; the artifacts are just as real in retrospect, and the gaps you find are just as instructive.

Part 2 · the why

The audit-trail discipline - why each artifact exists 7 min live

None of the seven artifacts is paperwork for its own sake. Each one answers a specific challenge that a reviewer, a supervisor, or in the worst case a misconduct panel can put to you - and the whole design goal is that any of them could walk your trail without you in the room. That is what defensible means. Not "I was careful" but "check for yourself."

LiveEvery session left you one piece - the callback tour4 min▶
  • b2 gave you the search log: strings, synonym harvesting, snowballing, and the known-set spot check that measures your string for free.
  • b3 gave you the screening record: criteria written before screening, PRISMA-style counts, and the verified-AI protocol whose trap you sprang in the lab.
  • b4 gave you the extraction table and its iron rule: every cell verified, AI suggestions marked, nothing on trust.
  • b5 gave you the analysis plan's spine: hypotheses before data, confirmatory vs exploratory marked before anything runs.
  • b6 gave you tests, seeds and CI on research code; b7 added the forking-paths discipline - Gelman & Loken (2013) is why the plan is written first - and together they produce the repro package.
  • b8 gave you the disclosure statement matched to your journal's current policy - which you verify against the live page, since these move.
  • b9 gave you the reviewer's eye - which day 5 turns on your own trail.

If any bullet feels unfamiliar, reread that session before capstone week - the capstone assumes them all.

Self-studyWhy disclosure is the artifact most worth over-doing3 min read▶

A 2025 arXiv analysis found only about 0.1% of post-2023 papers disclosed AI use, while roughly 70% of journals had policies asking for it - the widest gap between rule and practice anywhere in this course. That gap is an opportunity: a clear disclosure statement currently signals more care than almost any other sentence in a manuscript, precisely because so few write one. The capstone's disclosure names three things: what the AI did (tasks, not vibes), what it did not do (the analysis choices, the claims, the conclusions stayed human), and who verified each assisted step. Write it as you go during the week, not from memory on day 4 - retrospective disclosure is how honest people end up writing fiction.

Part 3 · the week

The five-day run order, time-boxed 5 min live

The week is designed against the two standard failure modes: day 1 perfectionism (still refining the search on Wednesday) and day 4 collapse (no time left to write or disclose). The time boxes are hard stops - when the box ends, you ship the artifact as it stands and note what is missing. An honest gap in the log beats a polished half-trail.

Demo · the ritual

The capstone walk-through ★ 15 min · everyone defends

Day 5, rehearsed today so the format is familiar. You present your trail to a partner in 15 minutes; the partner's job is to be politely hostile - they pull threads, you follow each one to its source in the artifacts, live. No slides, no narrative arc. Just the trail and the threads.

Minutes 0-3: state the question, the answer you currently believe, and the one honest unknown. Leading with the unknown sets the tone - this is an audit, not a pitch.

Minutes 3-6: the search-and-screen thread. Expect: "Show me the search string." "Run the known-set check in front of me." "This study looks relevant - why is it excluded?" Every answer is a pointer into the log or the record, not a recollection.

Minutes 6-9: the evidence thread. Expect: "Show me why this cell says N=48." You open the extraction table, follow the cell to its source, and the source agrees. If AI extracted it, the verification note is right there.

Minutes 9-12: the analysis thread. Expect: "Show me the seed." "Was this analysis confirmatory or exploratory - and when was it marked?" "Rerun it." The repro package answers all three or the gap goes on the list.

Minutes 12-15: the disclosure thread. The partner reads your statement and asks the only question that matters: "Is there any AI assistance in this project not named here?" Answer, on the record. Then swap chairs - playing the hostile reviewer teaches you as much as surviving one.

Real world

This ritual is a rehearsal for conversations that actually happen. A reviewer asking "how was this screened?", a supervisor asking "where did this number come from?", a panel asking "what did the AI do?" - the difference between an uncomfortable afternoon and a career problem is usually whether a trail exists. You are building the trail while it is cheap.

★ The one legal AI assist for day 5 - rehearse against your own disclosure

Here is my capstone disclosure statement and my list of artifacts (no unpublished data or manuscript text included): [paste the statement + the artifact list]. Act as a hostile methods reviewer. Generate the ten hardest questions you would ask in a 15-minute audit of this trail, ordered by damage potential. Do not answer them. Do not soften them.

Homework

The homework IS the capstone ◐ one week, time-boxed

  • Run the five-day plan on your real project. Book the boxes in your calendar now - day 5 first, because the walk-through needs a partner and a time, and a booked audience is what makes the week real.
  • Recruit your hostile reviewer: a labmate, ideally someone from this course, with this session's demo script as their brief. Offer to return the favour on their trail.
  • Keep a gap list all week: every artifact you could not finish, every cell you could not verify, every policy you still need to check against the live page. The list is a deliverable, not a confession.
  • After day 5: write the one-paragraph debrief - what held, what did not, and which single session of this track you will rerun on your next project. That paragraph is your personal syllabus from here.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · A reviewer asks: "How do I know your review of the literature did not simply miss the studies that disagree with you?" Which artifact answers, and how?

Each artifact defends one challenge, and coverage belongs to the search log. With the strings, databases, dates and the known-set check on the table, the reviewer can critique your actual coverage - and you can defend it - instead of trading assertions. The repro package defends the analysis; the disclosure defends the AI role; neither speaks to recall.

2 · Why must the confirmatory vs exploratory tag be applied BEFORE each analysis runs, rather than when writing up?

Gelman & Loken (2013) named the mechanism: with many defensible analysis paths, results-first labelling lets the path that worked get remembered as the plan. Dating the tag before the run is what makes "confirmatory" mean something. Exploration is welcome - the capstone only requires it be labelled as what it is, when it is.

3 · What is the one honest unknown FOR, in the deliverable set?

Every real project has a limit you cannot close - an unindexed corner of the literature, an unverifiable cell, a fragile assumption. Stating it plainly does two jobs: the hostile question arrives pre-answered, and the rest of the trail gains credibility, because a trail that admits its weakest point has visibly not been curated to hide it. b3's honest ceiling - the 12th study you cannot reach - was the same lesson in miniature.
