Writing and publication assistance: polish freely, disclose honestly

Every major publisher has now drawn the same line in roughly the same place: AI that fixes your grammar is polish and needs no announcement; AI that produces sentences you keep is generation and gets disclosed. This session walks the line publisher by publisher, tours the writing tools whose shapes are actually verified, states the equity evidence that makes polish tools a cause rather than a guilty secret - and then writes your disclosure statement in about a minute, because that is genuinely all it takes.

Part 1 · the line

Polish vs generation, rule-holder by rule-holder 8 min live

The landscape looks messy until you notice every policy is answering the same question: did the AI improve your sentences, or write them? Here is where each rule-holder draws that line - and because these pages move, verify the current version of your target journal's policy before submission day.

LiveThe disclosure gap: be the boring exception4 min▶

Here is the uncomfortable number: an arXiv analysis (2512.06705) found only about 0.1% of post-2023 papers disclosed AI use - while roughly 70% of journals had a policy asking for it. Whatever the true usage rate is, it is not 0.1%. The gap means a large share of the literature is quietly out of compliance with rules its authors could satisfy in one sentence.

Read the incentives correctly: right now, disclosure feels risky because it is rare, and it is rare because it feels risky. The way out of that loop is researchers who disclose routinely and blandly - a statement so boring it cannot be a scandal. That is the template in Part 3. An undisclosed use discovered later is a story; a disclosed one never is.

Self-studyWhy the line sits at generation, not at "any AI"3 min read▶

Publishers converged on this line for a practical reason: accountability tracks provenance. When a tool tightens your sentence, the ideas, claims and phrasing decisions remain traceably yours - the same reason human copy editors never appeared in author lists. When a tool produces sentences you keep, part of the text's provenance is a system that cannot be accountable for it, so the humans must say so and own it explicitly.

This is also why no publisher grants AI authorship (b1, a2): authorship is accountability, and a model cannot be accountable. Disclosure is not confession - it is the paperwork that keeps the accountability chain unbroken.

LiveThe equity evidence: polish is not cheating, it is leveling4 min▶

Worth stating plainly, with the numbers: Amano et al. (PLOS Biology 2023) found that non-native English speakers face 2.5x the rejection rates and 12.5x the requests for English revision. That is a tax on where a scientist was born, paid at every submission, and it has nothing to do with the quality of the science.

Polish tools are a legitimate equalizer against exactly that tax - and every publisher's polish allowance in Part 1 makes them policy-safe. A researcher whose results are sound but whose article-level English costs them a desk rejection is not gaming the system by using Writefull or Paperpal; they are removing noise from the signal reviewers are supposed to judge.

Real world

The line survives even here. The equity case covers polish - your sentences, improved. It does not convert generation into polish: a fully AI-drafted introduction is disclosed regardless of the author's first language. The strength of the equity argument is precisely that it does not need the rules bent.

Part 3 · the paperwork

Publication admin with AI in the room 5 min live

Three pieces of paper carry your AI honesty: the disclosure statement, the cover letter, and - when the reviews come back - the response letter. All three take minutes. Here is each, with its rule attached.

LiveThe one-minute disclosure statement3 min▶

Four ingredients: the tool and version, the task it did, and what the humans verified. That is the whole genre.

★ The template - fill in and go

During the preparation of this work the authors used [tool, version] to [task: e.g. improve the language and readability of author-written text / generate candidate phrasings for the introduction, which were revised]. After using this tool, the authors reviewed and edited the content as needed and take full responsibility for the content of the publication.

What a disclosure statement is not

"AI tools may have been used in parts of this manuscript." - no tool, no task, no verification, and "may have been" is not a disclosure, it is a shrug. Vagueness reads worse than the use itself.

Where it goes: Elsevier wants a statement in the manuscript for generative use; ICMJE wants the use described in the cover letter AND in the work - writing assistance in the acknowledgments, AI in the analysis in the methods. Your target journal's live policy page settles placement; verify the current version the week you submit.

LiveResponse to reviewers: the rules by extension, and the one red line3 min▶
  • No publisher-specific AI rules were found for rebuttal letters - a verified absence in this course's source check, not an oversight. The sensible reading: your manuscript's rules apply by extension, since the response letter is part of the same submission's record. Polish freely, disclose generation if your journal's policy would require it in the manuscript.
  • The red line is the input, not the output: never paste reviewer comments containing unpublished detail into consumer AI tools. Reviews quote your unpublished results and sometimes the reviewer's own unpublished reasoning; a consumer chat window is not a confidential channel (b1 rule 3, and the mirror image of the b9 reviewer bans).
  • Practical middle: draft your response points yourself, then let a polish tool tighten YOUR text - the reviewer's words never leave the journal system.
Self-studyThe cover letter line, ready to adapt2 min read▶

ICMJE asks for the use described in the cover letter as well as in the work. One sentence does it: "As disclosed in the acknowledgments, the authors used [tool] to [task]; all content was reviewed and verified by the authors." If your journal follows ICMJE (most medical journals do - check the current member and follower lists), the pair of sentences - one in the letter, one in the work - is full compliance. Two sentences, total.

Demo 1 of 2

The diff IS the authorship ★ 12 min · everyone builds

Bring one real paragraph from your current draft - methods or introduction work best. You will run a polish pass and then do the thing most people skip: read the diff line by line and decide.

Paste your paragraph into a polish tool (Writefull via Overleaf, Paperpal, Grammarly, or a general assistant prompted for language-only edits). Ask for improved language with your meaning unchanged.

Put original and revision side by side. Mark every change: word choice, sentence merge, reordering, anything added.

Accept or reject each change individually, out loud, with a reason. "Tighter" is a reason. "It changed my claim" is a rejection.

Find at least one change that subtly altered your meaning - hedges dropped, a "may" hardened into "does". These are why the diff is a gate and not a formality.

Classify the session: if everything you accepted was your sentences improved, that was polish. If it wrote sentences you kept, note them - they just earned a line in your disclosure statement.

★ Try it now - the polish-only instruction

Improve the language and readability of the following paragraph from my manuscript. Do not add claims, do not remove hedges or qualifiers, do not change any number or citation. Return the revision plus a list of every change you made and why. Paragraph: [paste yours]

Demo 2 of 2

Write your disclosure statement, check it against the live policy ★ 10 min · everyone builds

Not hypothetical: the manuscript you are working on now, the journal you actually plan to submit it to. Ten minutes from now this piece of admin is done for the rest of the project.

List every AI use in the project so far, honestly: polish passes, generated candidate text, coding help (b6), analysis assistance (b7). One line each: tool, version, task.

Sort each line: polish (no statement needed under Springer Nature / Elsevier-style rules) or generation and analysis (statement needed). When torn, disclose - it costs a sentence.

Write the statement from the Part 3 template, covering the disclosure-side lines. Add what the humans verified - that clause is what makes it read as diligence rather than confession.

Open your actual target journal's live AI policy page. Check placement (acknowledgments? methods? dedicated section?), cover-letter requirements, and anything stricter than the template assumes.

File the statement with the manuscript and date it. Re-verify the policy page the week you submit - these pages have moved before and will move again.

If your journal's policy page says nothing about AI Absence of a policy is not permission to skip the thinking - it means the publisher-level policy applies (Elsevier's and Springer Nature's cover their whole portfolios), and ICMJE applies if the journal follows it. Work to the strictest applicable rule per artifact, exactly as in b1, and keep your dated statement either way: the policy that arrives next year may ask about the paper you submitted this year.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Where does the disclosure line sit across the major publishers?

Springer Nature says AI-assisted copy editing of your own text needs no declaration; Elsevier exempts basic grammar and spelling but requires a statement for generative use; ICMJE wants the use described in the cover letter and the work. The shared logic is provenance: improved sentences are still yours, produced sentences are not - so the producers get named.

2 · What did Amano et al. (PLOS Biology 2023) find, and what does it mean for polish tools?

The study quantified the language tax on non-native speakers: 2.5x the rejections, 12.5x the revision requests, independent of the science. Every major publisher's polish allowance makes the fix legitimate. The equity case covers polish of your own sentences - it does not reclassify generation, which stays disclosed regardless of first language.

3 · What goes in a disclosure statement?

Specific and boring wins: name the tool and version, say what it did, and state that the authors reviewed the content and take full responsibility. "May have been used" is a shrug, not a disclosure; prompt logs are more than any policy asks. Placement follows your journal: statement in the work, one matching line in the cover letter where ICMJE applies.
