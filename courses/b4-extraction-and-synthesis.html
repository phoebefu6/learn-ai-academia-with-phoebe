Extraction and synthesis: the table IS the review

Your included set is decided (b3). Now every paper gets reduced to a row - population, design, N, measures, effect, limits - and that table, not your memory of the PDFs, is what your synthesis argues from. AI can draft the rows at remarkable speed; the peer-reviewed verdict is that it earns a place as a second reviewer, never as the only one. This session installs the one discipline that makes the speed safe: every AI-filled cell gets checked against the PDF before it enters your table, because a wrong N in your table becomes a wrong claim in your paper.

Part 1 · the table

Structured extraction, and the gate that keeps it honest 8 min live

A literature review that lives in prose lives in impressions. The extraction table is where impressions become checkable: one row per study, one column per thing your synthesis will need. Decide the columns before you read - they are your synthesis's data model.

LiveThe six columns your synthesis will need4 min▶

Columns vary by field, but six earn a place in almost every review:

ColumnWhat goes in the cellWhy the synthesis needs it
PopulationWho was studied, where, in what contextWhether findings transfer to your question's population
DesignExperimental, quasi-experimental, observational...How much weight the finding can carry
NThe analysed sample size, not the recruited onePrecision - and the cell most often silently wrong
MeasuresWhat was actually measured, with what instrumentWhether "the same outcome" is really the same
EffectDirection and size, in the paper's own termsThe raw material of every claim you will make
LimitsThe authors' stated limitations plus yoursWhere your one honest unknown will come from (Part 3)

The quiet rule: a cell records what the paper SAYS, with a page reference - "not reported" is a valid and honest value. Inference is synthesis work, and it happens in the matrix, not in the extraction table.

LiveThe tools, at their verified size4 min▶
  • Elicit is the extraction specialist: its Pro tier is documented for screening up to 5,000 papers and building tables up to 20 columns wide - a whole systematic-review workflow. Its headline accuracy numbers are self-published; the peer-reviewed literature (a Research Synthesis Methods feasibility study; Social Science Computer Review 2025) found it usable as a second reviewer, with human verification and iterative prompt refinement doing real work in both studies. Verify the current pricing page before you commit.
  • Covidence ships extraction suggestions and Rayyan ships relevance predictions - both vendor-documented assist features inside otherwise human-driven review platforms. Same rule applies: suggestions are drafts, and your protocol should say who verified them.

Notice what the honest framing changes in practice: you budget verification time INTO the plan instead of discovering it later. The tools save reading-to-find time; they do not save reading-to-confirm time. Demo 1 makes you feel that ratio on your own papers.

Self-studyVerify-every-cell: the mechanics3 min read▶

The discipline is simple enough to fit on a sticky note, strict enough to catch what matters:

  • Every AI-filled cell gets opened against the PDF before it enters your table - the number, the page, the sentence. Not a sample of cells: every cell. Sampling is how the wrong N survives.
  • Make the tool show its work. Ask for the supporting quote and location with every value. A cell with a quote takes seconds to verify; a bare number takes minutes - the prompt shape in Demo 1 builds this in.
  • Log corrections. Every cell you fix is a data point on the tool's error profile for YOUR corpus - tables and dense methods sections are where extraction typically strains. After 20 or 30 papers, your correction log tells you where checking must slow down.
  • Mark verification state. A simple tick per row: drafted / verified. Nothing unverified feeds the synthesis matrix, ever.
Part 2 · the citations

Citation context: what scite can tell you 5 min live

A citation count says a paper was cited; it does not say how. scite's Smart Citations classify the citing sentences themselves - as supporting, contrasting, or merely mentioning - which turns "cited 400 times" into a question you can actually ask: cited 400 times saying WHAT?

LiveThe boundary: verified capability vs your judgement4 min▶

Draw the line precisely, because tools blur it in their marketing:

  • What scite verifiably does: classify citing sentences as supporting, contrasting or mentioning. The verified anchor is the peer-reviewed system description - Nicholson et al. (QSS 2021), roughly 880M citation statements classified at that time. Whatever number the site shows today, treat as unverified until you check.
  • What that buys your review: a triage signal. A key paper in your table with substantial contrasting citations is a paper whose row deserves a second look - and those contrasting papers may belong in your review themselves. It is also an honesty check on your own writing: are you citing something as settled that the literature actively disputes?
  • What it cannot tell you: whether the paper is right (classification is not adjudication - a hundred supporting citations can share one bad assumption), and whether YOUR citation of it is apt (no tool reads your sentence and confirms the source says what you claim it says).

That last gap - auditing your own claims against your own sources, sentence by sentence - is a full discipline of its own, and it belongs to the AI + Research course in this series. This session points across rather than duplicating it: here, scite is a lens on the literature's argument, not a verdict on truth.

Part 3 · the argument

Synthesis that argues, not summarises 5 min live

A summary lists what each paper found. A synthesis argues: findings pattern BY study characteristics - the effect appears in designs like this, populations like that, and vanishes when measured the other way. The extraction table makes that argument possible; the synthesis matrix makes it visible.

LiveFrom extraction table to synthesis matrix4 min▶

The move is a pivot: rows stop being papers and become findings; columns become the study characteristics from your extraction table. Then you read the matrix for patterns:

  • Convergence: a finding that holds across designs and populations - the strongest thing your review can claim.
  • Moderation: a finding that appears under some characteristics and not others. This is where reviews earn their keep - the pattern IS the contribution, and only a structured matrix reveals it.
  • Contradiction: two verified rows that genuinely disagree. Do not average them in prose; ask what characteristic separates them. If nothing does, say so - that is a finding too.
  • The one honest unknown: every synthesis states what the evidence cannot yet answer. Your Limits column plus the matrix's empty regions hand it to you: the population nobody studied, the delay nobody measured, the comparison nobody ran. Naming it is not weakness - it is the sentence reviewers trust most, and often your next study.
LiveSource-grounded reading: interrogate YOUR corpus4 min▶

Source-grounded tools answer from documents you upload, with citations back to them. NotebookLM's free tier takes 50 sources per notebook and cites your uploads; Claude Projects offers a similar shape - your documents in, answers grounded on them. Two rules make them safe for synthesis work:

  • Grounding reduces errors; it does not eliminate them. That is Google's own framing in the NotebookLM FAQ, not a critic's. A grounded answer can still misread, overstate or stitch two sources into a claim neither makes - so an answer is a pointer to passages, and the passages are what you cite.
  • Never let it answer beyond your corpus. The moment a response draws on anything outside your uploads, you are back to leads-not-evidence territory (b2). The value here is interrogation: which of my fifty papers speak to X? Where do two of them disagree? What did I upload that I have not used? Demo 2 runs exactly this.
Real world

The contradiction question is the best first prompt for any corpus. Ask your notebook for the strongest contradiction between your included papers, then open both papers it names. One of three things happens: you find a real tension your matrix missed (excellent), you find the tool overstated a difference in framing (a grounding-limits lesson for free), or you find your own inclusion criteria let in two papers answering different questions (a b3 lesson, arriving late but welcome). All three outcomes improve the review.

Demo 1 of 2

Extract three of your papers, then verify every cell ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Not a toy exercise: these three rows are the start of your actual review table. You will time the AI half and the verification half separately - the ratio you feel today is the number your project plan needs.

Pick 3 papers from your included set - ideally one clean experiment, one messier design, one with a dense methods section. Have the PDFs open.

Start a timer. Run the extraction prompt below (in Elicit if you have it, otherwise any assistant that accepts your PDFs) for the six columns: population, design, N, measures, effect, limits. Stop the timer when three drafted rows exist.

Start the timer again. Verify every cell against the PDF: find the quoted sentence, confirm the number, mark each cell verified or corrected. All 18 cells - no sampling.

Count your corrections and note WHERE they happened - which column, which kind of paper. That is the start of your personal error profile for this tool on this literature.

Compare the two timer readings and write the ratio down. Drafting is fast; verifying is the real cost - and it is still usually far cheaper than extracting from scratch. Now your plan can budget for the whole method instead of the demo half.

The prompt that creates silent errors

Summarize these three papers into a table.

★ Try it now - extraction with the verification handles built in

For each attached paper, fill exactly these columns: population, design, N (analysed sample, not recruited), measures, effect (direction and size in the paper's own terms), limits (as stated by the authors). For EVERY cell: quote the exact sentence you took it from and give its page or section. If a value is not stated, write "not reported" - never infer, never estimate. Flag any cell you are uncertain about.

Demo 2 of 2

Interrogate your corpus for its strongest contradiction ★ 9 min · explore

Grounded tools are at their best asking questions OF your corpus, not questions beyond it. You will ask the sharpest question a synthesis needs answered - and then check the answer the way this session checks everything.

Load your included set (or today's best subset) into a NotebookLM notebook - the free tier takes 50 sources per notebook, which covers most review-sized corpora.

Ask: "What is the strongest contradiction between these papers? Name the two papers, state each one's finding, and quote the passages you are basing this on."

Open the two papers it names and read the actual passages. Is the contradiction real, or a difference in population, measure or framing that only LOOKS like disagreement?

If it is real: add it to your synthesis matrix as a contradiction and hunt the separating characteristic. If it is not: write one line on what the tool overstated - you just met the grounding limits first-hand, cheaply.

Ask one more: "What can these papers NOT answer about [your research question]?" Verify against your matrix's empty regions - between the two, your one honest unknown is usually staring at you.

The boundary to hold Everything the notebook says is a pointer into documents you can open - so open them. The moment you catch yourself citing the notebook's phrasing instead of the paper's, you have crossed from interrogation to delegation, and the session's first SVG shows where that dashed path leads.
Homework

Try it on your own review - this week ◐ 45-60 min total

  • Extend the extraction table to 10 papers with the same prompt and the same all-cells verification. Keep the correction log going - by row 10 you will know exactly which columns need slow checking on your literature.
  • Pivot the 10 rows into a first synthesis matrix (findings x study characteristics). Mark every convergence, moderation and contradiction you can see, and every empty region.
  • Draft your one honest unknown in a single sentence: what the included evidence cannot yet answer about your question. Pin it - b5 turns exactly this sentence into hypothesis material.
  • Look up two key papers from your table on scite. If either shows substantial contrasting citations, read one contrasting paper and decide whether it belongs in your review - that decision goes through your b3 criteria like any other.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Why does verify-every-cell insist on ALL cells rather than a spot-checked sample?

The failure mode is per-cell, not per-table: one wrong N becomes one wrong weight becomes one wrong sentence with your name on it, and no step in between raises an error. A sample catches the average error rate; the gate has to catch the specific error. That is why the discipline is every cell, with the quote and page requested up front so each check takes seconds.

2 · What is scite's verifiable claim, and where does it stop?

The verified anchor is Nicholson et al. (QSS 2021) - roughly 880M citation statements classified as supporting, contrasting or mentioning. That is a lens on the literature's argument, and a good one. Whether the paper is true, and whether your sentence uses it fairly, remain human judgements - the deep claim-vs-source audit discipline lives in the AI + Research course.

3 · NotebookLM answers a question with citations to your uploaded papers. What does grounding actually guarantee?

Grounding is a real improvement, which is why the tools earn a place for interrogating your corpus - and it still leaves room to misread a passage or stitch two sources into a claim neither makes. Demo 2's protocol is the answer in practice: ask the sharp question, then open the two papers it names. The tool finds candidates; the PDFs decide.
