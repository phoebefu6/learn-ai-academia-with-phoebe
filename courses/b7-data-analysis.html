Data analysis without forking paths: verify the code, count the choices

AI analysis tools run real code on your real data and hand back real-looking numbers - and the vendor's own documentation tells you to check them. This session builds the two disciplines that keep AI-assisted analysis publishable: a verify loop for every number the tool produces, and a forking-paths audit for every choice you made on the way to it. Because a tool that can try a hundred specifications in a minute is a wonderful assistant and a terrifying accomplice.

Part 1 · the tool

What AI analysis tools actually are 6 min live

ChatGPT's Advanced Data Analysis is not a model guessing at statistics from memory. It writes real Python, runs it in a sandbox on the file you uploaded, and returns the output. That is genuinely better than hallucinated arithmetic - and it moves the risk, not removes it. The code it wrote embodies choices you did not make and assumptions you did not state. OpenAI's own documentation instructs you to review the generated code, outputs and assumptions. That vendor sentence is this whole session in one line.

LiveWhy "it ran real code" is not the same as "it is right"4 min▶

A sandbox executes faithfully whatever the model decided to write. The classic silent choices, all observed in the wild and all invisible unless you read the code:

  • Missing values quietly handled - dropped, zero-filled, or mean-imputed, each of which is a methodological decision with a different effect on your estimate.
  • Outliers quietly trimmed - a "cleaning" step you never asked for, which is a results-changing exclusion rule wearing a helpful costume.
  • The convenient default test - a t-test where your design needs a mixed model, equal-variance assumptions nobody checked, the first significance threshold that came to mind.

None of these are crashes. The output looks exactly as authoritative either way - which is why the loop's step 2 is reading the code, not admiring the chart.

Self-studyThe one-number check: cheap, embarrassing, essential3 min read▶

Step 3 of the loop is deliberately minimal: pick ONE reported statistic and recompute it by an independent route - your own notebook cell, a spreadsheet pivot, a hand calculation on a small slice. If the group mean the tool reports does not match the group mean you compute, you have found either its bug or your misunderstanding of the data, and both are worth more than the plot.

One number is not a full audit, and is not meant to be. It is a tripwire: it converts "I trust the tool" into "I have evidence about this specific output", at a cost of two minutes. Your repro package (b6) is the full audit - the tripwire decides whether the output deserves to enter it.

Part 2 · the accelerant

The garden of forking paths, now with an engine 8 min live

Gelman & Loken (2013) made the uncomfortable point that multiple comparisons are a problem even without intentional fishing: every analysis involves choices - exclusions, transforms, covariates, outcome definitions - and each choice silently multiplies the paths through the data. A researcher who honestly walks one path still walked a path that the data helped choose. AI turns that garden into a highway: a tool that can try a hundred specifications in a minute industrializes researcher degrees of freedom.

LiveThe discipline: confirmatory, exploratory, and the count between them4 min▶

The defence is not abstinence; it is bookkeeping with teeth:

  • The preregistered analysis plan decides what is confirmatory. The plan you wrote in b5 - before seeing outcomes - names the one path whose p-values mean what p-values claim to mean.
  • Everything else is exploratory and says so. Exploring is legitimate science; presenting exploration as confirmation is the misconduct. The label is the whole difference.
  • Specification counts get reported. "We examined 14 model specifications; the preregistered primary analysis is Table 2; the remainder appear in the supplement" is one sentence, and it converts a suspicious pattern into an honest method.

Notice what this makes of AI's speed: with the labels in place, a tool that runs many specifications fast becomes a robustness check - the same result across 90 of 96 paths is evidence of stability. Without the labels, the identical run is a p-hacking machine. The discipline, not the tool, decides which one you own.

Self-studyThe AI-specific literature has already named this3 min read▶

This is not a hypothetical the course invented - published commentary has been mapping the AI version of researcher degrees of freedom:

  • Prompt-Hacking (arXiv 2504.14571) - the prompt itself becomes a researcher degree of freedom: rephrase until the analysis flatters you.
  • Sanity Checks for Agentic Data Science (arXiv 2604.11003) - agentic analysis pipelines need explicit guardrails precisely because they explore specification space autonomously.
  • LLM-hacking in annotation (arXiv 2509.08825) - when an LLM does your labeling, model and prompt choices propagate into downstream conclusions like any other analytic fork.

Different corners of the pipeline, one shared finding: every place an AI makes a choice for you is a fork you must either fix in advance or disclose. Note these are arXiv commentary and preprint-stage work - read them as the field thinking out loud, and verify the current versions before citing them yourself.

Part 3 · the workflow

The honest analysis workflow, with AI in it 5 min live

The stable division of labour: AI assists exploration and mechanics; you own models, inference and claims. EDA assist - plots, summary tables, data-quality checks - is low-risk and high-value, because its outputs are inputs to your judgement rather than statements in your paper. Model choice and inference stay yours. Every AI-produced figure gets its code read before it gets believed. And the repro package from b6 captures the final path, so the analysis that appears in print is the one anyone can re-run.

LiveThe anti-pattern gallery: three moves that end careers slowly4 min▶
Anti-pattern 1 · the specification slot machine

"That wasn't significant - try log-transforming the outcome. Still not? Try excluding participants over 65. Try adding the interaction term. Keep going until p < .05."

Anti-pattern 2 · outsourcing the judgement call

"Is this result publishable? Which of these analyses would reviewers find most convincing?" - the model will answer, confidently, and the answer is worthless: publishability is a claim about truth and standards, not about text.

Anti-pattern 3 · the silent cleanup

"Clean the data and rerun" - and the tool removes outliers you never saw, under a rule you never chose, changing an estimate you are about to publish. Silent outlier removal is an exclusion criterion that skipped peer review.

All three share a signature: the human asked for an outcome and let the machine choose the path. The honest workflow reverses it - the human chooses the path, the machine does the walking.

Demo 1 of 2

Run the verify loop on a real upload ★ 12 min · everyone builds

Feel the loop once with your own hands and it becomes reflex. Use a SAFE dataset: a public one (a government open-data table, a classic teaching dataset) or your own non-sensitive data. The privacy gate is part of the demo, not an obstacle to it.

Pick your safe dataset and confirm it passes the b1 gate: no participant data, nothing unpublished that is not yours, nothing a collaborator would be surprised to find in a consumer tool.

Upload it to an AI analysis tool and ask for EDA: distributions of the key variables, a missingness summary, and the three data-quality problems it would flag first.

Open the generated code and actually read it. Write down every decision it made that you did not ask for - filtering, imputation, type coercion, binning.

Pick one reported statistic - a group mean, a correlation, a count - and recompute it yourself in a notebook or spreadsheet, from the raw file. Compare.

Say the assumptions out loud: complete the sentence "these outputs are correct only if..." with at least two conditions the code silently assumed. That sentence is a draft of your methods section.

★ Try it now - EDA with the receipts attached

Here is a dataset [attach safe file]. Do exploratory analysis only: distributions of each variable, missing-data summary, and the top three data-quality issues. For every step: show the code you ran, list every row you excluded or altered and why, and state the assumptions your choices rely on. Do not fit any models. Do not remove outliers without listing them.

Demo 2 of 2

The forking-paths audit of YOUR analysis ★ 10 min · everyone builds

Not a toy exercise: bring the analysis you are actually running this month. You will leave knowing your own path count - most people are surprised by their number, and the surprise is the point.

List every choice point in your current analysis: exclusion rules, variable transforms, covariate sets, outcome definitions, subgroup splits, the significance threshold itself.

For each choice point, count the defensible versions - the ones a reasonable colleague might have picked. Not the absurd ones; the plausible ones. Honesty beats flattery here.

Multiply. That product is your garden. A modest analysis with 4 choice points of 3 options each is already 81 paths.

Mark which choices were fixed BEFORE you saw the data - in your prereg plan (b5), your protocol, or a dated note. Those forks are closed; they are what "confirmatory" means.

For every fork still open, write the one-line rule that closes it now, dated today - or accept that the result it touches is exploratory and will be labeled so. Both are honest; only silence is not.

If your path count scares you Good - it should, and it is the same count it was yesterday; you just measured it. The fix is not deleting analyses, it is labeling: one preregistered path reported as confirmatory, the rest as exploratory with the specification count stated. Reviewers respect a stated garden far more than a suspiciously tidy single path.
Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 40-50 min total

  • Finish the forking-paths audit if you ran out of time, and file it with your prereg plan. It is the appendix your future methods section will thank you for.
  • Run the verify loop on the next three AI-produced outputs in your real work, and keep a tally of what step 2 (reading the code) caught. Your personal catch rate is the best argument for the habit.
  • Add one sentence to your current draft's methods: how many specifications you examined and which one was preregistered. Feel how cheap honesty is when it is one sentence.
  • Check whether your target journal or field has reporting guidance on analytic flexibility (registered reports, robustness sections) - verify the current version of whatever you find before relying on it.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What does OpenAI's own documentation say about Advanced Data Analysis outputs?

The vendor's own instruction is the session's thesis: the sandbox guarantees the code ran, not that the code was right for your question. Reading the code catches silent choices, the one-number check catches silent errors, and stating assumptions turns hidden defaults into methods. The tool's maker asks for exactly the loop this session builds.

2 · Gelman & Loken showed forking paths bias results even without intentional fishing. Why does AI make this worse?

The original problem was a human wandering the garden one path at a time, guided by the data. AI removes the friction: all the paths, walked in minutes, prettiest result surfaced. The choices still multiply silently - now at machine speed. The same speed becomes a robustness check the moment the confirmatory path is fixed in advance and the count is reported.

3 · What makes a result confirmatory rather than exploratory?

The label is about timing, not tooling. A path chosen before seeing outcomes (b5's plan) yields p-values that mean what they claim; a path chosen after - by you or by an AI - is exploration, which is legitimate and valuable exactly when it is labeled. Robustness across many specifications is nice, but it is evidence of stability, not a substitute for the pre-commitment.
